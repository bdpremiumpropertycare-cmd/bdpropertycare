Grass Cutting Done Right: The One-Third Rule and Why Regular Visits Win

Reviewed against University of California IPM, University of Minnesota Extension and Warwickshire County Council lawn guidance. Last updated 2026.

A neat lawn is mostly a matter of how it is cut, not just how often. The most widely repeated rule in lawn care is also the simplest, and it is the main reason regular, scheduled visits tend to beat the occasional big chop.

The One-Third Rule

The University of California's Integrated Pest Management programme gives the standard guidance: take no more than a third of the grass blade off in a single cut. Removing just that much keeps the lawn healthy, while removing more risks stunting its growth.

In practice this sets a ceiling on how long grass can be left between cuts. If the mower is set to leave grass at one height, the lawn needs cutting before it grows to one and a half times that height. Leave it longer and you either break the rule or scalp the lawn.

Why Overgrown Lawns Need Patience

When a lawn has been left, the temptation is to cut it straight back to a neat height in one pass. Lawn guidance from University of Minnesota Extension, reported in a Master Gardener column, advises the opposite: set the mower high, remove the top third, give the grass a few days to recover, then cut again slightly lower. It takes more than one visit, but it avoids shocking the lawn.

Leave the Clippings Where They Fall

Warwickshire County Council's guidance on mulch mowing makes a related point. Following the one-third rule is what allows clippings to be mulched back into the lawn rather than bagged, and it recommends mowing at a higher setting. The Extension guidance above also suggests leaving clippings on the lawn rather than bagging them. Fewer, smaller clippings means less waste and a tidier finish.

What This Means for Recurring Visits

  • Regular beats occasional: frequent cuts at a sensible height keep each cut within the one-third limit.
  • Height matters as much as frequency: a slightly higher cut slows growth and makes it easier to stay on schedule.
  • Neglected lawns are a project, not a single job: bringing grass back under control may take two or three visits.

Small Repairs, Same Logic

The same thinking applies to small property repairs. Minor issues such as worn paintwork or a leaking gutter section tend to develop gradually and are cheaper to deal with early, a pattern we describe in our article on blocked gutters.
