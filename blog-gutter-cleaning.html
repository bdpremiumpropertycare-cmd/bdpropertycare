Gutter Cleaning

What Clogged Gutters Actually Do to Your Property

Reviewed against guidance from the US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) and the US Environmental Protection Agency (EPA). Last updated 2026.

Gutter cleaning is easy to postpone — nothing looks visibly wrong until the damage is already underway. But the function of a gutter system is simple and important: it channels rainwater off the roof and away from the building's foundation. When that channel is blocked, the water has to go somewhere else, and the consequences are well documented.

The Volume Problem

It's worth appreciating how much water a roof actually sheds. Even moderate rainfall over a typical roof surface adds up to a substantial volume of water that has to go somewhere. When gutters are clear, that volume is carried safely to ground level and away from the building via downspouts. When they're blocked with leaves and debris, that same volume overflows directly down external walls and pools at the foundation instead.

Foundation and Structural Risk

Water pooling repeatedly at a foundation saturates and erodes the surrounding soil. Over time this can contribute to foundation settling and cracking, and — if the water finds its way through porous foundation walls — damp or flooding in basements and crawl spaces. Gutters are also a physical structure: standing water and trapped debris add weight, which can pull gutters away from the roofline or cause them to sag and fail.

Roof and Interior Damage

Blocked gutters cause water to back up under the roof edge rather than draining away, which can damage roofing materials, rot fascia boards, and allow water to penetrate the roof structure, leading to leaks and interior water damage. In colder conditions, blocked gutters can also worsen the formation of ice dams, which force meltwater back under roofing materials.

A Documented Mould and Moisture Risk

This isn't just anecdotal. The US Environmental Protection Agency's own published guide on residential mould control lists moisture prevention actions homeowners should take, and specifically instructs residents to "clean and repair roof gutters regularly" as a core mould-prevention measure, alongside grading the ground to slope away from the foundation. Mould control, per the EPA's own guidance, starts with moisture control — and gutters that aren't doing their job are a direct route for that moisture to reach the building.

A Recognised Mosquito Breeding Site

Standing water in a blocked gutter is also a mosquito breeding site. Public mosquito-prevention guidance — including material published by the US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) — consistently instructs residents to eliminate standing water in and around the home as a core mosquito-prevention measure, and local health authorities that build their guidance on CDC methodology routinely list clogged roof gutters by name as a common breeding site to check and clear.

Why Regular Clearing Matters More Than Reactive Fixes

Most of the damage above develops gradually and isn't visible from ground level until it's already significant — a crack in a foundation wall, a damp patch on an interior ceiling, or a sagging gutter line. Scheduled clearing, ideally at least annually and more often where a property is close to trees, is the straightforward way to avoid all of the above becoming a repair bill.
